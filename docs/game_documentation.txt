# Clash of the Cities - Mission Net Zero
## Player Game Guide, Rules, and Sustainability Knowledge

## 1) Game Purpose
Clash of the Cities - Mission Net Zero is a real-time cooperative simulation where players run a city waste and recycling ecosystem.

Your team objective is to keep the system healthy and sustainable by balancing:
- **Budget ($)**
- **City Health (%)**
- **CO₂ Emissions (tCO₂e)**

The game teaches circular economy thinking: recover value from waste, reduce landfill dependence, and cut emissions while maintaining economic performance.

---

## 2) Team Roles and Responsibilities
Each game has 3 roles. Success depends on coordination.

### 2.1 Municipality
Main focus:
- Collect waste efficiently
- Manage city-level priorities
- Support city projects and public outcomes

Typical responsibilities:
- Handle incoming waste flow and dispatch decisions
- Prevent backlog and missed collection windows
- Keep city health stable by reducing unmanaged waste and poor system conditions

### 2.2 MRF (Materials Recovery Facility)
Main focus:
- Sort and process waste into useful materials
- Improve material quality and reduce contamination

Typical responsibilities:
- Process incoming batches quickly
- Convert waste into tradable material inventory
- Avoid long queue delays and process bottlenecks

### 2.3 Broker
Main focus:
- Manage market activity and auction outcomes
- Turn recovered materials into economic value

Typical responsibilities:
- List and trade materials strategically
- Optimize timing and pricing decisions
- Balance revenue opportunities against inventory and timing risk

---

## 3) Core Game Flow (What Happens During a Match)

## 3.1 Session Start
When a game starts:
- Team roles are assigned
- Initial budget and city health are set
- Initial waste appears
- Real-time game clock begins

## 3.2 Continuous Real-Time Loop
During the session, the game repeatedly:
1. Spawns/updates waste pressures
2. Applies penalties/bonuses from current system state
3. Recalculates city health and related metrics
4. Broadcasts state updates
5. Checks countdown/game-over conditions

This means your actions have immediate and compounding effects.

## 3.3 Typical Player Interaction Cycle
A practical cycle looks like this:
1. **Municipality** manages collection flow and system pressure
2. **MRF** processes and grades materials
3. **Broker** sells/trades outputs through market mechanisms
4. Team reviews Budget + Health + CO₂ trend
5. Team adapts strategy before the next wave/pressure

---

## 4) Core Systems Players Must Understand

### 4.1 Waste Batches
Waste arrives in batches with properties like:
- Source/origin
- Mass
- Composition
- Time/deadline pressure
- Status (pending/processed/etc.)

If batches remain unmanaged too long, system penalties increase.

### 4.2 Queues and Throughput
Backlogs reduce performance.
- Long pending queues create pressure on city outcomes
- Delayed processing can hurt health and overall stability

### 4.3 Material Recovery and Quality
Higher-quality recovered materials usually create:
- Better sale opportunities
- Better system efficiency
- Better long-term team performance

Contamination and poor sorting reduce value and sustainability performance.

### 4.4 Budget Dynamics
Budget changes through:
- Operating costs
- Processing/handling outcomes
- Material sales and market actions

If budget collapses, countdown-to-loss risk can start.

### 4.5 City Health Dynamics
City health reflects overall environmental and operational success.
Health typically worsens when:
- Waste is unmanaged/overdue
- System bottlenecks persist
- Emissions and poor flow compound

Health can recover with strong operational control and coordinated actions.

### 4.6 CO₂ Emissions
CO₂ accumulates from operational activity, transport, and system inefficiency.
Lower CO₂ generally indicates better circular performance and more resilient strategy.

---

## 5) Win/Lose and Game-Over Logic (Player View)

## 5.1 Immediate Risk Triggers
Loss countdown risk starts when either condition is reached:
- **City Health <= 0**
- **Budget <= 0**

## 5.2 Countdown Window
When countdown starts, your team has a short rescue window.

Current implemented countdown values:
- Countdown duration: **30 seconds**
- Recovery from health crisis requires: **Health > 5**
- Recovery from budget crisis requires: **Budget > 1000**

If recovery does not happen before countdown expiry, that team is eliminated/lost.

## 5.3 Time-End Condition
A match also ends when full game time is reached.
At that point, pair/team final status is computed based on outcome logic.

## 5.4 Practical Meaning for Players
You lose when you allow critical system collapse to persist.
You win by keeping your system viable while the opposing side fails first or by finishing with stronger final outcome.

## 6) Practical Strategy for Players

### 6.1 Team Strategy Priorities
1. Prevent emergency thresholds (health/budget collapse)
2. Keep waste moving (avoid backlog)
3. Protect material quality
4. Convert inventory into timely revenue
5. Reduce unnecessary emissions

### 6.2 Municipality Tips
- Avoid over-accumulation of pending waste
- Keep collection flow stable instead of reactive spikes
- Coordinate with MRF before batches become critical

### 6.3 MRF Tips
- Prioritize high-impact queues to avoid penalty cascades
- Preserve quality (contamination control)
- Convert waste to usable material quickly and consistently

### 6.4 Broker Tips
- Do not sit on inventory too long in volatile moments
- Use market timing to support team cash stability
- Align selling decisions with team crisis state, not only peak price

### 6.5 Crisis Playbook (When Countdown Starts)
If countdown reason is health:
- Rapidly reduce waste pressure and stabilize processing flow
- Improve system state until Health > 5

If countdown reason is budget:
- Execute fast monetization decisions
- Cut avoidable losses and restore Budget > 1000

## 7) Circular Economy Knowledge (For In-Game Decision Making)

## 7.1 Circular vs Linear Economy
- **Linear model**: take -> make -> dispose
- **Circular model**: recover -> reuse -> recycle -> regenerate

The game rewards circular behavior: keeping materials in productive loops and reducing waste leakage.

## 7.2 Why Circular Operations Improve Game Outcomes
Circular strategies typically:
- Reduce landfill pressure
- Preserve material value
- Improve economic resilience
- Lower emissions across the system

## 7.3 Key Circular Principles
1. **Waste is a resource** when properly sorted and recovered
2. **Material quality matters** for reuse and resale value
3. **System coordination matters** more than isolated optimization
4. **Prevention beats correction** (avoid backlogs before they become crises)

---

## 8) Sustainability and Carbon Knowledge

## 8.1 Why CO₂ Matters
CO₂ is a performance signal for climate impact.
In this game, lower CO₂ usually means better system design and logistics efficiency.

## 8.2 Major Emission Sources in Waste Systems
- Transport of waste/materials
- Processing energy use
- Inefficient handling and repeated movement
- Disposal pathways that miss recovery opportunities

## 8.3 Lower-Carbon Operating Patterns
- Shorter, cleaner material loops
- Fewer avoidable transfers
- Faster processing and less idle stock
- Higher recovery rates and lower contamination

## 8.4 Health, Economy, and Climate Are Linked
A strong team does not optimize only one metric.
Best outcomes usually come from balancing:
- Financial viability (Budget)
- Public/environmental performance (Health)
- Emissions control (CO₂)

## 9) Player FAQ (RAG-Ready)

### Q1: What should I do first when the game starts?
Coordinate role priorities immediately: keep flow stable, process early batches fast, and avoid early budget/health shocks.

### Q2: What causes sudden collapse?
Ignoring backlog, delayed processing, or poor market timing until health or budget falls into emergency range.

### Q3: What is the most important metric?
No single metric. You must jointly manage budget, health, and CO₂.

### Q4: How do we recover during countdown?
For health countdown: push health above 5.
For budget countdown: push budget above 1000.
Do it before 30 seconds expires.

### Q5: Why does coordination matter so much?
Because each role controls different parts of one shared system; local optimization can hurt team-level outcomes.

### Q6: What is a circular economy in one sentence?
It is an economic approach that keeps materials in continuous use while minimizing waste and emissions.

---

## 10) Quick Reference Checklist
Before every major decision, ask:
1. Does this protect health from critical thresholds?
2. Does this protect budget from collapse?
3. Does this reduce or at least not unnecessarily increase CO₂?
4. Does this improve end-to-end flow across Municipality -> MRF -> Broker?
5. Does this help the team avoid emergency countdown conditions?

If most answers are yes, it is usually a strong move.
