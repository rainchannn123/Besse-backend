# Clash of the Cities - Mission Net Zero
## Player Game Guide (Latest Public Version)

This document is for players. It explains gameplay, team flow, and scoring logic only.

---

## 1) Game Objective
Your team must keep the city system stable and competitive by balancing:
- **Wallet ($)**
- **City Health (%)**
- **CO₂ Emissions (tCO₂e)**
- **Score**

You win by making better circular-economy decisions than other teams in the same room.

---

## 2) Match Format and Team Capacity
- A game room can host **n teams (up to 30 teams)** in the same match.
- Each team has 3 roles:
  1. Municipality
  2. MRF (Materials Recovery Facility)
  3. Broker
- Teams compete in parallel under the same room timer and conditions.

---

## 3) Role Responsibilities

### 3.1 Municipality
- Collect and route waste.
- Start and complete city projects.
- Coordinate with MRF for recovery and return logistics.

### 3.2 MRF
- Process waste into usable materials.
- Maintain quality and reduce contamination.
- Choose proper destination flow, including **go-to-landfill** when needed.

### 3.3 Broker
- Sell materials through available market channels.
- Participate in auctions under fair-trade rules.
- Convert inventory into wallet value efficiently.

---

## 4) Core Flow for New Players
1. Municipality collects and routes waste.
2. Waste reaches MRF for sorting/processing.
3. MRF can:
   - Recover material for market use, or
   - Send selected waste to **landfill** (usually lower-value, higher-impact fallback).
4. Materials and requests move between **Municipality ↔ MRF** as needed.
5. Broker lists/sells/auctions output.
6. Team metrics (Wallet, Health, CO₂, Score) update continuously.

---

## 5) New/Updated Gameplay Rules

### 5.1 MRF Landfill Option
MRF now has a **go-to-landfill** action path.
- Useful for handling specific waste situations.
- Overuse can hurt sustainability performance.

### 5.2 Revised Municipality Project Rewards
Municipality project rewards were revised.
- Completing projects now has updated reward impact.
- Good project timing can stabilize both wallet and score.

### 5.3 Two-Way Transportation (Municipality and MRF)
Transportation now supports both directions:
- **Municipality -> MRF**
- **MRF -> Municipality**

This adds tactical logistics decisions (speed, queue pressure, and emissions impact).

### 5.4 Auction Participation Restriction
A team **cannot bid on its own listed auction**.

### 5.5 Auction Settlement and Agent Fee
When an auction is completed:
- Winner pays the bid amount.
- Seller receives payment.
- **10% market agent fee** is deducted by the marketplace.

## 6) Metric Variation Logic (Player Formula View)
These are player-facing equations for understanding direction of change.

### 6.1 Wallet Variation
Wallet_next ≈ Wallet_now + SalesIncome + ProjectRewards - OperatingCosts - LogisticsCosts - MarketFees

### 6.2 Health Variation
Health_next ≈ Health_now - BacklogPenalty - DelayPenalty - PollutionPenalty + RecoveryActions + SuccessfulCityOps

### 6.3 CO₂ Variation
CO2_next ≈ CO2_now + TransportEmissions + ProcessingEmissions + LandfillImpact - EfficiencySavings

### 6.4 Score Variation
Score_next ≈ Score_now + SustainabilityGains + EconomicPerformance + OperationalStability - CrisisPenalties

Interpretation:
- Faster clean flow usually improves Health and Score.
- Efficient transport and less landfill dependence usually improve CO₂ outcomes.
- Better selling + lower avoidable costs improve Wallet.

---

## 7) Win/Lose Basics
- Teams must avoid collapse conditions (for example, severe Wallet/Health failure states).
- Teams that stay stable longer and perform better across metrics rank higher.
- Strong results come from balancing all four outcomes, not maximizing only one.

---

## 8) Beginner Playbook
- Keep waste moving; avoid queue build-up.
- Use landfill sparingly and strategically.
- Coordinate transport timing between Municipality and MRF.
- Complete high-value projects at the right time.
- In auctions, remember fee impact and never expect self-bidding.
- Watch Wallet, Health, CO₂, and Score together before every big decision.

## 11) Advanced Learning Appendix: Circular Economy Concepts Mapped to Game Features

### 11.1 Circular Economy in This Game
This game models a circular system where materials should stay in use as long as possible.

In practical gameplay terms:
- **Better route**: Waste -> MRF processing -> reusable materials -> projects/market
- **Fallback route**: Waste -> landfill

Circular actions generally produce stronger long-term outcomes because they:
- preserve material value,
- reduce repeated extraction/purchase pressure,
- reduce unmanaged waste pressure,
- and usually keep both wallet and city systems more resilient.

### 11.2 Waste Hierarchy (Applied)
Use this decision order during play:
1. **Prevent delays** (collect and process quickly)
2. **Recover materials** (maximize useful output)
3. **Reuse in city projects** (convert to city benefits)
4. **Trade strategically** (auction / wholesaler decisions)
5. **Landfill only when necessary** (last-resort pathway)

### 11.3 Why Logistics Design Matters
The game intentionally links logistics to sustainability:
- More transport actions can improve flow speed, but also add cost and CO₂.
- Slow/fast mode is a strategic trade-off (cheaper vs faster).
- Bad timing can cause queue build-up, deadline penalties, and weak project completion tempo.

### 11.4 Material Quality and Value Recovery
MRF output and quality decisions affect market value and project readiness.
This reflects real systems: contamination and low quality reduce downstream reuse value.

### 11.5 Multi-Team Competition and Shared Learning
With up to 30 teams in one room, players can learn from market behavior:
- aggressive bidding can solve short-term shortages but hurt wallet,
- excessive saving can miss critical project windows,
- efficient teams usually synchronize Municipality + MRF + Broker decisions.

## 12) Detailed Public Mechanics (Current Implemented Values)

### 12.1 Match and Timer Parameters
- Team match duration: **15 minutes**
- Waste spawn interval target: **every 0.5 minutes (~30s)**
- Waste batch deadline before overdue penalty: **2 minutes**
- Overdue batch penalty: **-7 Health per overdue batch**

### 12.2 Starting Team State
- Starting Wallet: **$20,000**
- Starting City Health: **60**
- Starting CO₂: **0**

### 12.3 Transport (Municipality <-> MRF)
Transport modes currently implemented:
- **Fast:** $50 per ton, ~20 seconds
- **Slow:** $25 per ton, ~40 seconds

Transport emissions:
- **+1.6 tCO₂ per transport trip**

### 12.4 Landfill and Processing Values
- Standard landfill factor: **2.5 tCO₂ per ton landfilled**
- Dumping fee baseline: **$50 per ton**
- MRF direct landfill option currently applies reduced factors:
  - cost based on ~70% of estimated processing disposal cost
  - landfill CO₂ at ~50% of standard landfill factor for this specific action

### 12.5 Waste Spawn Mass and Origins
Typical spawned batch mass:
- **10.0 to 25.0 tons** (randomized)

Origin compositions:
- Residential: paper 50%, plastic 30%, glass 20%
- Commercial: paper 40%, plastic 40%, metal 20%
- Industrial: plastic 40%, metal 30%, wood 30%

## 13) Equation Library (Player-Facing, Implementation-Aligned)

### 13.1 Core Transport and Emissions
1. **Transport Cost**
   - Fast mode: `Cost = BatchMass × 50`
   - Slow mode: `Cost = BatchMass × 25`

2. **Transport CO₂**
   - `TransportCO2 = Trips × 1.6`

3. **Landfill CO₂ (standard)**
   - `LandfillCO2 = LandfilledMass × 2.5`

### 13.2 MRF Processing Output
For each material type in a batch:
- `InputMass(material) = BatchMass × Composition(material)`
- `OutputMass(material) = InputMass(material) × ProcessRate(material)`
- `RefuseMass(material) = InputMass(material) × WasteRate(material)`

Aggregated:
- `TotalRefuse ≈ BatchMass - Sum(AllOutputMass)`
- `DumpingFee = TotalRefuse × 50`
- `ProcessingCO2 = BatchMass × 0.015`

### 13.3 Material Price and Grade Logic
- `MaterialValue = BasePrice × Mass × QualityMultiplier`

Quality multipliers:
- Material grades: A = 1.25, B = 1.00, C = 0.50
- Waste grades (where applicable): B = 0.30, C = 0.20, F = 0.10

### 13.4 Health Pressure Rules
1. **Waste threshold penalty**
   - If pending waste > 100 tons:
   - `WastePenalty = (PendingWaste - 100) × 1%`

2. **CO₂ threshold penalty**
   - If total CO₂ > 200 tons:
   - `CO2Penalty = floor((TotalCO2 - 200) / 50) × 1%`

3. **Project bonus component in health model**
   - `ProjectBonus = CompletedProjects × 5%` (system model component)

### 13.5 Auction Rules and Settlement Equations
- Auction duration: **30 seconds**
- Bid increment rate: **5% of entry price** each bid
  - `NextBid = CurrentBid + (EntryPrice × 0.05)`
- Seller cannot self-bid on own auction.

Settlement on winning bid:
- Winner pays full bid amount.
- Seller payout rate = 90%
- Market fee = 10%

Formulas:
- `SellerPayout = FinalBid × 0.90`
- `MarketFee = FinalBid × 0.10`
- `WinnerWalletNext = WinnerWalletNow - FinalBid`
- `SellerWalletNext = SellerWalletNow + SellerPayout`

### 13.6 External Wholesaler Equation
- `ExternalPurchaseCost = BasePrice × 2 × RequestedAmount`

## 16) Ranking and Competition Clarification (Current Behavior)

For live multi-team competition:
- Team ranking is computed by **total completed project score**.
- Wallet, Health, and CO₂ remain critical for survival and action capacity.
- A team with strong project completion usually ranks higher, but poor Wallet/Health control can still cause elimination risk.

Practical interpretation for players:
- Build score through project completion,
- while maintaining operational stability to avoid being knocked out early.

## 17) Advanced Beginner Strategy (Learning-Oriented)

### 17.1 First 3 Minutes
- Prioritize stable waste intake and avoid missing early deadlines.
- Start low/medium projects that match your likely material recovery flow.

### 17.2 Mid-Game
- Use MRF processing to feed both auction opportunities and municipality project needs.
- Do not overuse landfill unless it prevents a bigger collapse.

### 17.3 Auction Discipline
- Remember every bid step is fixed from entry-price increments.
- Include 10% marketplace fee effect when estimating seller return.

### 17.4 CO₂-Aware Planning
- Prefer fewer unnecessary movements.
- Improve timing so material sits less in queues.
- Use high-impact projects to offset pressure with stronger rewards.

### 17.5 Circular Economy Learning Outcome
A strong city team applies all three principles together:
1. **Operational flow control** (avoid delay/backlog)
2. **Resource recirculation** (recover and reuse)
3. **System-level optimization** (wallet + health + emissions + score)
